01 / The problem

Good advice is not the same as a completed task.

Advertisers had to navigate multiple tools and technical decisions to connect data and prepare campaigns. An assistant could explain a setup method, yet still send the advertiser into a fragmented manual journey.

For the Signals MVP, I focused on three gaps: guidance without enough account context, setup spread across product surfaces, and low confidence about why a recommendation mattered or whether the action had succeeded.

02 / The approach

Ground the experience in what the system can reliably do.

I worked with the team to clarify the advertiser journey, system boundaries, and MVP behavior. We connected Signals domain knowledge with the assistant’s capabilities, reviewed design directions, and refined the workflow for engineering.

  1. 01 / GuideRecommend a relevant setup path.
  2. 02 / ValidateHelp the advertiser understand the current state.
  3. 03 / ResolveMake the next action clear and achievable.
  4. 04 / ImproveConnect setup with ongoing signal quality.

03 / Key decisions

Reduce the work, not the advertiser’s control.

01

Bring supported actions into the assistant.

Replace a separate manual setup path with an in-assistant action where the integration supports it. Keep the recommended method and the action together.

02

Meet advertisers in their current task.

Surface recommendations alongside the workflow advertisers already use, rather than expecting them to discover a separate AI destination.

03

Make the answer specific to the account.

Structure guidance around the current setup, relevant signal gaps, recommended data sources or events, and the value of taking action.

04

Place help at the decision point.

Add contextual AI entry points where advertisers need an explanation to understand a choice and decide what to do next.

04 / Measurement

Measure the conversion, not the conversation.

The Signals workstream’s measurement plan focused on whether advertisers connected useful data and improved its quality. Conversation activity alone was not the outcome.

Integration adoption

Active Conversions API and Meta Pixel integrations.

Signal quality

Event Match Quality and Event Coverage improvements.

Revenue was tracked downstream as a business outcome, not treated as proof that a single interaction change caused the result.

05 / Outcomes & reflection

A Signals contribution to a broader AI product.

My contribution was the Signals MVP’s interaction model: connecting account context, understandable guidance, and supported setup actions. The figures below are published product-level context, not results attributable to my workstream alone.

20%

Higher resolution rate for common account issues in the assistant’s early beta.

12%

Lower cost per result after advertisers applied opportunity-score recommendations, which are available in the assistant.

Source: Meta’s April 22, 2026 announcement ↗. These describe different product-level outcomes and are not a controlled evaluation of the Signals MVP.

What I learned

Trust comes from aligning the explanation with the action the system can actually support. The useful unit of design is the advertiser’s completed workflow, not an isolated chat response.
